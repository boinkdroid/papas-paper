The Cherry Dip Cone, Explained at Last

“I was so busy looking at her I walked it right into the door.”

For forty years the family has heard pieces of it at Thanksgiving. On Tuesday, Papa told the whole thing.

It was the summer of 1958, the Dairy Queen on Main Street had just put in a dip machine, and he had exactly one quarter. "I bought the cone, turned around, and there was your grandmother," he said. "I was so busy looking at her I walked it right into the door."

The cherry coating went down the front of his only good shirt. She laughed so hard she had to sit down on the curb. He asked her to the VFW dance anyway.

"She said she'd think about it," he said. "She thought about it for two weeks."

How Papa’s Doing

Fixing the Gutter, Again

Papa spent the morning on the garage gutter, which he reports is "fixed for good this time." Lunch was soup with Earl. He says his knee is better than last week.

Forty Acres and a Milk Cow Named Duchess

Before school, every morning, there was Duchess. Papa was eight when the milking became his job.

"She'd kick the bucket over if your hands were cold," he said. "So you learned to warm your hands."
