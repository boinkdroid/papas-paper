From the sample edition · Told out loud, set in type

DECATUR, Ill. — Every evening at six, a small newspaper lands in the inboxes of the people who love him most. He wrote every word of it. More precisely, he said every word of it, into his iPhone, earlier that afternoon.

The app is called Papa’s Paper, and it has two big buttons. One says Answer a question, and it asks him something worth answering: who taught him to drive, what the kitchen smelled like on Sundays, what he did the day he got home from the service. The other says Tell a story, and simply listens.

When he’s done, the app asks a short follow-up or two, the kind a curious grandkid would ask. Then one last question: put it in tonight’s paper, or keep it private? Private means private. It is never sent to anyone.

At six o’clock his time, the day’s stories are written up as a proper newspaper, headlines and all, in his words, and emailed to everyone on his list.

Nobody in the family has to download anything. They read it like any other email, tap a reaction, or write back. Their notes show up on his phone the next time he opens it.

If he tells a story he has told before, the paper notices and runs it as a revisit rather than a rerun. On days he has nothing to say, no paper goes out. Nobody gets a blank page.

Inside this edition

For the family

Readers Need Nothing but an Email Address

The family doesn’t install a thing. He adds your email address, and you get a short welcome note, then the paper on any day there’s a story to tell.

Not on the list yet? Every paper has a link to ask for it. He gets a big card on his phone that says you’d like to read it, and he decides.

Privacy

His Stories Stay His

His recordings are used to write his paper and nothing else. No ads, nothing sold, and the AI services we use don’t train on what he says.

Stories he marks private are never sent to anyone. He can delete his whole paper from the app at any time, and it’s gone for good. Read the privacy policy.

Design

Built for Big Thumbs and Long Stories

Large type, plain words, two buttons. No menus to get lost in and nothing to type while he’s telling a story.

He can hear any paper read back to him, and he can tell his stories in English, Spanish and eight other languages.

From the editor

Why There’s a Paper at All

I made Papa’s Paper for my grandpa. He has more stories than any of us ever had time to hear in one sitting, and he’s been planning to tell the big one, his whole life, a chapter at a time.

I wanted those stories to have somewhere to land: a little every day, where the whole family could read them and write back. If you have someone like him, I hope it does the same for yours.

Catie, granddaughter and editor
