How it works

From their armchair to your inbox

Grandma or Grandpa tells the story. We set it in type. Everyone reads it with dinner.

  1. Step 1They talk

    They tap Answer a question or Tell a story and just talk. The app asks a follow-up or two, like a curious grandkid would.

  2. Step 2We write it up

    At 6 PM their time, the day’s stories become a real little newspaper, with headlines, in their own words.

  3. Step 3Family writes back

    Everyone on their list gets it by email. One tap to react, or write back. They see every note on their phone.

For grandparents

So simple they’ll figure it out before you finish explaining

Big type, plain words, and nothing hidden in menus. If they can answer the phone, they can make a paper.

Two big buttons

“Answer a question” or “Tell a story.” That’s the whole home screen.

Good questions

Who taught you to drive? What was Sunday dinner like? It asks out loud, and listens.

Nothing to type

Talk, then tap “I’m finished.” No keyboard and no spelling.

Reads it back

Every paper is saved in the app, and one button reads it aloud.

Their language

Tell stories in English, Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese and five more.

Their name on top

Nana’s Paper, Grandpa Joe’s Paper, Abuela’s Paper. Each paper is named for whoever tells the stories.

Privacy

Their stories stay theirs

They decide what goes in the paper and who reads it. Read the privacy policy.

Private means private

After every story they pick: tonight’s paper, or keep it private. Private stories are never sent to anyone.

No ads. Nothing sold.

Recordings are only used to make their paper. The AI services we use don’t train on them.

Delete anytime

One button in the app deletes the whole paper and every recording, for good.
